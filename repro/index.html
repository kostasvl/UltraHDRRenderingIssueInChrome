Tile seams on gain-map (UltraHDR) images

  1. Select two or more UltraHDR / gain-map JPEGs (any photos from a recent Android phone with HDR on). At least one should have large smooth light areas — a plain wall, sky or ceiling — otherwise the lines are hidden by detail.
  2. Press ← / → to step between them.
  3. Watch the arriving image for thin dark lines. They fade away on their own after about a second.
  4. Change the height with , / . and step again.

A static image does not reproduce this. The seams appear on the freshly rasterized image arriving from a transition, and resolve on their own shortly after.

Whether they appear depends on the render height: some heights are consistently affected and others are consistently clean, and a one-pixel change can flip it. The position and thickness of the lines change with the height too.

Firefox shows no seams on the same files — but Firefox does not support UltraHDR, so it is displaying the SDR base image rather than a composited gain-map result, which makes it a weak comparison rather than a true control.
